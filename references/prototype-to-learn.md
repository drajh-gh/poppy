# Prototype to learn

Use a prototype when a bounded artifact can answer a material product, design, logic, or technical-feasibility question more directly than discussion alone.

## Decision contract

Before creation, record:

- the decision and uncertainty or hypothesis;
- evaluator and representative scenarios;
- observable answer signal;
- required fidelity;
- scope and time boundary;
- authorized effects; and
- retention or disposal plan.

Support behavior or logic prototypes, experience or UI prototypes, and technical-feasibility spikes. Use project language and include awkward, illegal, boundary, and failure scenarios when they could change the decision.

## Isolate the artifact

Default to clearly marked disposable artifacts with in-memory, synthetic, stubbed, or scratch state. Real routes, data, authentication, providers, or production-like effects require a bounded safety and authority assessment. Redact sensitive state and expose only what evaluation needs.

Placement, file count, language, and test strategy follow the decision and project constraints. Do not mandate an adjacent file, single HTML document, or absence of tests. Use question-proportionate checks.

## Evaluate and close

Run the predeclared scenarios and separate observation from inference. Execution alone is not validation. Close the question as:

- answered;
- contradicted;
- partial; or
- unverified because the deciding evidence is missing or inadequate.

Record the evidence, limitations, remaining risks, and next decision. Dispose of or retain artifacts only according to the authorized plan. Inventory prototypes, fixtures, recordings, profiles, and helpers; never delete them automatically.

Production implementation starts as a fresh Delivery decision. Rewrite or harden the selected behavior under normal project conventions and verification. Never directly promote prototype code, automatically create a branch, commit, issue, context note, or memory entry, or treat prototype success as production readiness.
